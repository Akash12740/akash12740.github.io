TOPIC 01 • ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE 5 MIN READ

From Symbolic Rules to Agentic Workflows: Designing Reliable AI Systems

Modern Artificial Intelligence has evolved rapidly from static prediction endpoints into autonomous, tool-using agents capable of planning multi-step tasks. However, deploying AI in enterprise environments fails when teams treat probabilistic models as deterministic software.

The 3-Layer Reliability Architecture

  • Deterministic Guardrails: Wrap stochastic model outputs with strict schema validation, input sanitization, and confidence thresholds before executing downstream actions.
  • Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Escalation: Route low-confidence edge cases to human reviewers while logging failure traces for continuous dataset improvement.
  • Closed-Loop Evaluation: Measure system-level task completion rates and safety compliance rather than relying solely on offline benchmark scores.
User Intent ──▶ Input Guardrails ──▶ Reasoning / Tool Execution ──▶ Verifier Gate ──▶ Action or HITL
Key Takeaway: Production AI reliability is an engineering systems problem—combining mathematical bounds, deterministic verification, and continuous evaluation.
TOPIC 02 • MACHINE LEARNING 6 MIN READ

The Mathematics of Generalization: Regularization, Bias-Variance & Hyperparameter Tuning

Training a classifier to achieve 99% accuracy on a training split is easy; ensuring it maintains 95%+ accuracy on unseen real-world distributions is the true craft of Machine Learning. When building my Fake News Detection NLP pipeline, disciplined regularization made the difference between overfitting noise and learning generalizable linguistic signals.

Why Mathematical Foundations Matter in ML

  • L1 vs. L2 Regularization: Adding a penalty term to the loss function constrains parameter norms, preventing high-dimensional TF-IDF n-gram features from memorizing rare tokens.
  • Stratified K-Fold Cross-Validation: Preserves class proportions across every validation fold, eliminating optimistic data leakage during GridSearchCV.
  • Margin Maximization: Online linear classifiers like Passive-Aggressive algorithms update weights only when margin violations occur, yielding fast, sparse, and robust text boundaries.
Objective: min_w [ (1/N) Σ L(y_i, wᵀx_i) + λ₁‖w‖₁ + λ₂‖w‖₂² ] ──▶ 95.0% Out-of-Sample Accuracy
Key Takeaway: Before reaching for heavier neural architectures, strong feature engineering paired with regularized linear models and stratified validation often delivers superior speed and interpretability.
TOPIC 03 • DATA SCIENCE 5 MIN READ

Why 80% of Data Science Impact Happens Before Model.fit()

In academic competitions, datasets arrive cleanly formatted in a single CSV. In real-world Data Science, signal is buried inside noisy, duplicated, and inconsistently encoded raw logs. Great data scientists treat data ingestion and hypothesis formulation as first-class engineering problems.

End-to-End Scientific Workflow

  • Distribution & Drift Auditing: Check missingness mechanisms (MCAR vs. MAR), heavy-tailed outliers, and covariate shift between historical and current windows.
  • Deterministic Preprocessing Pipelines: Encapsulate cleaning, imputation, and scaling inside reproducible pipelines so training and inference transformations never diverge.
  • Metric Alignment with Business Risk: Choose asymmetric loss metrics (e.g., Precision-Recall AUC or MAPE) when false positives and false negatives carry vastly different operational costs.
Raw Unstructured Logs ──▶ Schema & Anomaly Checks ──▶ Feature Store ──▶ Hypothesis Testing ──▶ Model
Key Takeaway: A simple model trained on clean, de-duplicated, domain-aligned data will consistently outperform a complex model trained on noisy pipelines.
TOPIC 04 • DATA ANALYTICS 5 MIN READ

Turning Raw Operational Metrics into Executive Decisions with SQL & Time-Series EDA

Data Analytics bridges the gap between raw database tables and strategic action. Whether auditing loan transaction anomalies in PostgreSQL/MongoDB or decomposing 15 years of state power demand, clarity comes from isolating signal from seasonal noise.

Practical Analytics Playbook

  • Cohort & Funnel Decomposition: Aggregate metrics alone hide churn; slicing KPIs by acquisition cohort, region, and time window exposes root causes immediately.
  • Window Functions in SQL: Use LAG(), LEAD(), and rolling AVG() OVER (ROWS BETWEEN 6 PRECEDING AND CURRENT ROW) to compute moving baselines and detect sudden anomalies.
  • Seasonal Decomposition (STL): Separate long-term trend, recurring seasonal cycles, and residual variance before setting operational forecasts or SLAs.
Observed Series Y_t = Trend (T_t) + Seasonality (S_t) + Residual Noise (E_t)
Key Takeaway: Effective analytics doesn't just describe what happened—it isolates the specific lever decision-makers need to pull next.
TOPIC 05 • LARGE LANGUAGE MODELS (LLMs) 6 MIN READ

Mitigating LLM Hallucinations: Instruction Fine-Tuning, RAG & Citation Grounding

Large Language Models are trained to predict plausible next tokens, which makes them fluent—but prone to confident fabrication when queried on private or specialized knowledge. Drawing from my experience curating Gemini instruction fine-tuning workflows at Innodata and building FAISS RAG pipelines, mitigating hallucinations requires a two-pronged defense.

Two Pillars of Trustworthy LLMs

  • High-Signal Instruction Tuning & Safety Audits: Curating adversarial edge cases and teaching models when to abstain ("insufficient context") drastically reduces unsupported claims during post-training.
  • Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) with Similarity Gates: By chunking verified documents with overlap, indexing dense embeddings in FAISS, and enforcing minimum cosine similarity thresholds plus mandatory [doc_id | chunk] citations, every claim remains auditable.
Query ──▶ Dense Embedding ──▶ FAISS Top-K (Score ≥ Threshold) ──▶ Strict Citation Prompt ──▶ Grounded Answer
Key Takeaway: Combining post-training alignment with retrieval similarity gates transforms LLMs from creative text generators into verifiable enterprise knowledge engines.

CASE STUDY 02 • TIME-SERIES & ANALYTICS 5 MIN READ

Why SARIMA Outperformed ARIMA by 60% RMSE on 15-Year Energy Demand

Standard autoregressive models often fail on multi-year utility datasets because power consumption exhibits strong 12-month seasonal cycles alongside long-term economic drift.

SARIMA(1, 1, 1) × (1, 1, 1, 12) ──▶ RMSE: 164.7 MWh | MAPE: 2.38% (vs. AR RMSE: 412.8 MWh)

Using Augmented Dickey-Fuller (ADF) stationarity testing and seasonal differencing (D=1, s=12), SARIMA captured recurring summer/winter load peaks that non-seasonal ARIMA smoothed over—reducing out-of-sample RMSE from 412.8 to 164.7.
