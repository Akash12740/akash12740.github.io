INTERACTIVE LLM AUDIT

Spot the AI Reasoning Flaw

Problem: Let A ∈ M₄(ℝ) have minimal polynomial m(t) = t³ - t. How many connected components can G = {X ∈ GL₄(ℝ) | AX = XA} have?

Model Trace A: Eigenvalues {0, 1, -1} have multiplicities {2,1,1}, so G ≅ GL₂(ℝ) × GL₁(ℝ) × GL₁(ℝ). Since each GL_n(ℝ) is connected, G has 1 component.
Model Trace B: G ≅ GL₂(ℝ) × GL₁(ℝ) × GL₁(ℝ). Each GL_n(ℝ) splits into det > 0 and det < 0 (2 components), giving 2 × 2 × 2 = 8 components.

06. MATHEMATICAL_FOUNDATIONS_OF_AI

Fascinating Facts About Math & AI

FACT 01 • FORMAL REASONING

From Olympiad Proofs to Lean 4

AI mathematical reasoning jumped from silver-medal formal translation in 2024 to gold-medal natural-language proofs at IMO 2025 (35/42) and perfect 42/42 scores at IMO 2026—paired with formal verifiers like Lean 4 that check every logical step with 100% mathematical certainty.

FACT 02 • VECTOR GEOMETRY

Why Cosine Similarity = Dot Product

In FAISS vector databases, normalizing embedding vectors to unit L2 length (‖u‖ = ‖v‖ = 1) turns angular cosine similarity into a simple inner product (u·v)—cutting retrieval latency by orders of magnitude across millions of chunks.

FACT 03 • HIGH DIMENSIONS

Blessing & Curse of Dimensionality

In 768-dimensional transformer embedding spaces, two random vectors are almost always nearly orthogonal (~90°)—which is why semantic meaning clusters cleanly into distinct subspaces without colliding.
